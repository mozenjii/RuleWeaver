# R2 - #18 VC / market analyst

## 1. Will anyone pay?
Barely, and not yet. All 20 reports say "would not buy today"; #01, #02, #03 and #11 offer only a free shadow run. Every price is hypothetical. The most credible buyers have an objective oracle and a dated need: #09 UK calculator vendor (GBP 8-15k pilot, April uprating, parity against his live calculator); #02 and #05 US benefits nonprofits ($10-25k, $25-60k); #06 integrator IV&V ($40-80k, but that is hourly services). #03 tax ($50-150k/yr) has the biggest budget and the hardest access. Nobody asked for software. What recurs in #01, #06, #08, #09, #11 and #12 is amendment-impact and independent verification of rules they already run. That, not "signed rule packs", is the demand signal.

## 2. Disagreements
- **#07 (legal-tech PM):** Re-point to HR compliance plus monitoring and an obligation register. That is the most crowded category (GRC, change monitoring, LLM-native entrants), and two real payroll buyers contradict it: #01 ("I already employ those people"; "unknown" is wrong on payday) and #08 (official algorithms; parameters, not prose). #04's "obligations-register mode" is the same scope creep.
- **#19 (OSS model):** "Content is the moat." His own caveats say the law is public and encodings have thin copyright; a pack is protected only by contract. The moat is the update relationship.
- **#17 (cross-border):** Sole proprietor, no entity. #03, #06, #15 and #16 name entity, liability cap and insurance as entry tickets; #16 doubts a Pakistan sole proprietor is insurable.

**Changed my mind:** #01 and #08 killed my own HR/payroll suggestion. #20 and the verified facts show the review gate is unwired (faults never seeded, edit broken, 6 of 10 parameters empty). My "differentiator is copyable" was too kind; today it is unproven.

## 3. Attack on the consensus
- **Sign-off paradox.** Buyers with budget keep accountability: #02 ("the signature to be mine"), #04, #06, #10, #11. Buyers who need outside sign-off lack budget, and an attorney's hours eat a $10-25k pilot.
- **Ordering is capital-inefficient.** "Entity, insurance, security pack first" costs roughly $12-43k on the reports' own estimates (pentest $5-20k, insurance $2-8k, legal $5-15k) before a $10-25k invoice. Add 6-12 month TPRM cycles (#04, #05, #11) and net-45/60 with retainage (#06).
- **"SNAP is only a demo"** abandons the one built asset to start eight domains. Each needs new ingestion, reviewers and an IR that #20 says is shaped by one fixture.

## 4. Final call
**Domain:** US state-level SNAP (income, deductions, allotment) for one state. **Buyer:** a benefits-access nonprofit or benefits-tech vendor (#02) that already runs a calculator or PolicyEngine. **Delivery:** self-hosted, fixed-fee, sold as independent verification plus October/January change-impact reports, with the buyer's calculator as the oracle. Their analysts own the signature, so no credentialed signer is needed. eCFR is already built; the new work is state-manual HTML/PDF ingestion and filling the empty parameters.

Sequence: employer IP release (free), then a signed LOI with a 50% deposit (#17), then entity and insurance only as that buyer requires.

**Stop:** platform engineering (hosted API, metering, OpenFisca polish, more diagnostics), and every "reviewed" claim until the gate is wired.

**Verdict:** Unchanged on equity (no; bootstrap). Changed on product: a verification and change-impact service, not rule packs. HR wedge withdrawn.
