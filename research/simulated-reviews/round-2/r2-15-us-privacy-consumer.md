**3. Attack the consensus.** Weakest part: "buyers pay for credentialed-signed rule packs." It is the hardest product to insure, describe truthfully and sell. Sign-off does not move accountability (#11, #10). It makes a part-time founder warrantor to unlimited customers, and it invites UPL claims. SNAP currency is unverified after the 2025 law (#02), so "approved against the regulation" may be stale on day one. The $10-40k pilots are only safe if the deliverable is a bounded, dated artefact with disclaimed warranties (diff/impact report, trace pack), not an assurance.

**4. Final call.** UK, Universal Credit core, self-hosted, sold to benefits-calculator vendors as a capped-liability services contract: uprating plus amendment-impact report, no household data (#09). From my US seat, the US is the costliest first market: SNAP confidentiality, state privacy laws, offshore clauses, state UPL rules. England and Wales reserve few legal activities (outside my lane; verify). **Stop:** asserting "reviewed", "approved" or "anti-rubber-stamping" anywhere (NOTICE string, README) until the code and a named signer back it.

**Verdict:** still no-buy, but sharper. The problem is present-tense claims, not future compliance.
