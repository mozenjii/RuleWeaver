# R2 - #10 EU digital-government Innovation Lead

## 1. Is it needed, will anyone pay?
Not demonstrated. Nearly every report says "would not buy today", and 00 notes all prices are simulated. Most credible: #09 (UK calculator vendor): GBP 8-15k Universal Credit pilot, GBP 10-25k/yr pack. Councils and charities buy under tender thresholds, legislation.gov.uk is structured, and uprating plus amendment impact is a recurring, checkable job. Second: #02 (US nonprofit), $10-25k, but grant-funded and PolicyEngine is free. #12's GBP 20-50k works only as a subcontract under a firm that supplies the signers. My agency would not pay; I could fund EUR 15-50k of grant-style work. Realistic outcome: a funded public good or small niche, not a software business.

## 2. Disagreements
- **#19 (OSS model)**: "sell packs protected by contract, no foundation." Encodings of my law that I commission are public goods; I'd demand an open licence, and I won't pay a vendor to attest my own law. A host organisation isn't waste; it is what lets grants and public buyers contract at all.
- **#17 (cross-border)**: "no entity, Pakistani sole proprietor, services only." Workable for a US private pilot; fatal for EU public buyers. Directive 2014/24 selection criteria (Art. 58) and ESPD apply, and I believe a bidder from a country outside the GPA/trade agreements has no treaty right to equal treatment (Art. 25), so I can simply exclude it. An EU/UK prime is required.
- **#14 (AI Act)**: "enter the EU via tax/VAT." Tax administrations are the most mature in-house Rules-as-Code users (to my knowledge, Catala at the French tax administration, RegelSpraak in Dutch tax), so the founder is most redundant there. And #01/#03/#20 want the OpenFisca export cut: for public buyers it is the one interop bridge, and AGPL (00 #4) bothers governments that publish code far less than private vendors.

**Changed my mind:** #14: my claim that the review gate evidences Art. 14(4)(b) was overreach (Art. 14 binds overseers of a deployed system), and "solely by natural persons" weakens when an LLM drafts. #13: GDPR Art. 22 covers deterministic calculators, so my H4 fight was the wrong one; also hash chain vs Art. 17 erasure. 00 items 1-3: seeded faults never run, "edit" fails, 6 of 10 parameters are empty. I called LLM authoring behind hard checks "genuinely distinctive"; it is unmeasured, so only "unknown" survives.

## 3. Attacking the consensus
"Buyers pay for credentialed-reviewer-signed packs" is the weakest part. The signer is the one asset the founder lacks and cannot cheaply buy; #16/#17 doubt the founder is even insurable; #18 admits it becomes a services business. For public buyers it inverts: interpretation is mine, not a vendor's. "Entity, insurance, security pack first" contradicts #17 (premature) and spends money before evidence. The $10-40k pilots come from buyers whose own conditions (state or UK coverage, benchmark, signer) are all unmet. And the compile step is not the proven value: #20 says the tool wins only on re-review after amendments, #05 says compilers pay at thousands of rules. The measurable product is amendment impact.

## 4. Final call
- **Domain/buyer/model:** UK Universal Credit core plus annual uprating; buyer UK calculator vendors, charities and councils (#09); self-hosted or in-browser rule pack with amendment-impact reports; a UK/EU host partner supplies a named welfare-rights reviewer; a parallel grant (NLnet/NGI-type, availability uncertain) funds open legislation.gov.uk point-in-time ingestion, a stepping stone to Akoma Ntoso.
- **Stop:** asserting trust properties the code doesn't deliver ("named reviewer", seeded-fault catch rates, ADR-022's "not an AI system"). In EU procurement, serious misrepresentation is an exclusion ground (Art. 57(4)(h), I believe).
- **Verdict:** direction unchanged (not buying; grant-fund with an EU/UK host), but harsher on the product as shipped. Art. 14 claim withdrawn.
