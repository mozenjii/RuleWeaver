# R2 - #06 US govtech integrator

## 1. Does anyone pay?
Not for software. Every "buyer" said "I would not buy today" and priced only a pilot. The most credible, because each has an existing paid workflow:
- **#03 tax CTO:** $15-40k pilot, then $50-150k/yr. The only figure that funds a company, but season-gated and needs a CPA signer.
- **#02 nonprofit / #09 UK lead:** $10-25k / GBP 8-15k. Real pain, small budgets.
- **#12 Big-4:** GBP 20-50k with the founder as subcontractor. This is my own model: I pay for named people on funded work, not tools.

All prices are simulated. At these sizes it is consulting income, not a company (#18).

## 2. Disagreements
- **#17:** "No US entity now; sole proprietor plus W-8BEN." Fine for a private pilot. For my channel it fails: I cannot onboard a foreign sole proprietor into state vendor registration, insurance certificates and flow-downs.
- **#07:** "Lead with HR/employment compliance, the founder's own domain." HR operations is not a counsel credential, and #01 says payroll vendors compete with their own compliance teams.
- **#15:** Offshore access is "a procurement problem, not a legal bar." For the prime it is a bar: Pub 1075 and work-location clauses end the bid.
- **Changed my mind:**
  - **#20 and orchestrator facts 1-3** (seeded faults unwired, edit broken, shipped package returns "unknown"). I withdraw my round-1 advice to lead with seeded faults as IV&V hooks.
  - **#12's independence point.** Formal IV&V must be independent of the prime's build (verify the CMS/FNS rule), so I am the wrong channel. Sell to IV&V firms or a state PMO.
  - **#02/#18:** CFR may lag statute; state manuals and FNS memos are the real source.

## 3. Attack on the consensus
The weakest link is the **credentialed reviewer**. Most reports demand one; none says who pays, who carries liability, or who insures it. At my guess of $150-400/hr, a $20k pilot buys 50-130 hours before the founder's own time. That is negative margin, and #16 doubts a carrier will insure a Pakistan-based sole proprietor.
The sequencing is also backwards: entity, insurance, pentest and security pack (roughly $15-40k summing #15/#16) before one signed LOI. Get a funded partner first (#17).
"SNAP is only a demo" is right for 15 federal rules but wrong as a domain. The reports scatter the founder across payroll, tax, EWA, MAGI, UK UC and EU law, where he has no signer and no corpus.

## 4. Final call
**US, one state's SNAP (income, deductions, allotment, state options), for a benefits-screening nonprofit or vendor (#02 profile), as a self-hosted fixed-fee verification pack.** Include test vectors, a dated October change-impact report and a hired credentialed benefits analyst as reviewer, benchmarked against PolicyEngine and the state's worksheets. That pilot becomes past performance; then come to me for a teaming role.

**Stop:** treating the hosted API, metering and OpenFisca polish as product, and adding any new domain or jurisdiction, until that pilot is paid.

**Verdict:** still "would not buy today", but harder. I withdraw the IV&V pitch, and my $40-80k state-pilot figure is a price the founder cannot yet earn.
