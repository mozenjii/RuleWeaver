# R2 - #11 EU bank, Head of Regulatory Compliance Technology

## 1. Does anyone pay?
Nobody pays for software; every report prices the engine at zero. The only prices I believe are #1's (payroll VP, $20-60k/yr), anchored to displacing 0.25-0.5 of a compliance engineer, and #4's (neobank compliance head, $40-120k/yr per product line), because a compliance budget line and an exam use case exist. I discount #3's $50-150k (season-gated, incumbents) and every pilot figure ($10-50k): that is discretionary innovation money, not recurring demand. All figures are simulated. A few pilots are plausible; a business is unproven.

## 2. Disagreements
- **#16 (security):** "answer N/A, self-hosted" and "NIS2/SOC 2 not needed". Not at a bank. DORA applies to me directly, and rule-pack updates and support access are ICT services wherever the code runs. Self-hosting changes the GDPR role, not my register of information.
- **#17 (cross-border):** entity and insurance spend is "premature". For an EU financial buyer an identifiable legal entity is a precondition (my register wants a provider identifier, normally an LEI). A Pakistani sole trader cannot be onboarded, so the entity comes before the pilot.
- **#18, #09, #12:** legislation.gov.uk as the EU/UK entry point. Useful for benefits and payroll, wrong for finance, whose operative text is in EUR-Lex, EBA/ESMA standards, the FCA Handbook and PRA Rulebook.

**Changed my mind:** #4 and #7. Most of a bank's obligations are duties, not computations, and the tool extracts only "computable" clauses, so it skips my largest supervisory exposure. My round-1 CCD2/AMLR slice cannot carry a product. Also verified facts 1, 2, 6 and 7: I credited clause-level trails as supervisory evidence, but an unkeyed chain, one shared secret minting any reviewer, unwired seeded faults and a NOTICE naming nobody make the trail a misrepresentation risk in my due diligence.

## 3. Attacking the consensus
The weakest claim is "buyers pay for credentialed-reviewer-signed packs". A regulated buyer's accountability does not transfer by signature (DORA Art. 5, management body), and I would sign off myself anyway, so a vendor signature is an input worth less than it costs. The reviewer plus E&O is the cost base, a solo founder cannot underwrite the liability, and nobody priced it: that is a consultancy with consultancy margins (#18).

Meanwhile #1, #4, #6, #7, #8 and #12 each name amendment-impact analysis or semantic diff as what they would use. The consensus buried its one convergent purchase driver under a service the founder cannot deliver.

Also, "pilot first, security pack later" is backwards for regulated buyers. Onboarding costs more than the pilot fee, so pilots must run on public text, non-production, no personal data.

## 4. Final call
- **Domain:** US consumer-finance obligations, Reg E/Z/DD (12 CFR 1005/1026/1030), already in eCFR so no new ingestion. They include annually adjusted dollar thresholds, which are parameter-style content. I am confident in that general point but have not checked specific figures.
- **Buyer:** compliance head at a fintech or neobank (#4).
- **Delivery:** self-hosted batch tool for regulatory-change impact and an obligation register, public text only, no customer data, with a law-firm partner for review.
- **Not EU banks, and not me:** revisit after a US reference and a DORA due-diligence pack exist.
- **Stop:** asserting named-reviewer sign-off and anti-rubber-stamping before the code and a qualified reviewer exist.

**Verdict:** still no purchase, and harder than round 1. I withdraw my free proof-of-concept offer until the approval gate is real and the audit trail resists forgery.
