# R2 - #02 US benefits-access Head of Product

## 1. Will anyone pay?
Yes, but small and services-shaped. Most credible: #06 (integrator), who prices independent verification of legacy eligibility rules hourly inside a bid, plus a $40-80k one-state pilot; and #05 (enrollment navigation), $25-60k pilot and $20-60k per state-year. #09's GBP 8-15k pilot needs UK code that does not exist. I distrust #03's $50-150k/yr (CPA signer, season-gated). Nobody pays for software. My own $10-25k is the weakest figure: nonprofits spend restricted money; I am a design partner, not the market.

## 2. Disagreements
- **#07 and #01 (HR/payroll wedge).** #01 and #08 call "unknown" unusable on a payslip. Unknown pays only where input is naturally incomplete: screening and enrollment.
- **#18 and #19 (verified content is the moat).** #19 concedes thin copyright in encodings, and PolicyEngine already maintains state SNAP free.
- **#06 ("unknown is table stakes").** True for case-management pend states; in screening, most calculators default a missing fact to zero.

**Changed my mind:** #20. The shipped SNAP package returns "unknown" on the headline answer and no faults are ever seeded, so my free-evaluation offer was premature. #09 and #07: hosting is fine for public-text monitoring, and in-browser execution suits nonprofits that cannot self-host. I retract "self-host only".

## 3. Attacking the consensus
The weakest link is the economics of "signed packs plus chassis first". #15 and #16 together imply roughly $12-43k in year-one legal, pentest and insurance cost against $10-25k pilots, before paying any credentialed reviewer. #17 calls entity and insurance spend premature for a small private pilot; I side with #17. Second, "SNAP is only a demo" is backwards: it is the one domain with a free competitor and an annual update event. The FY2027 adjustments took effect around 1 October (my recollection; verify), so the shipped tables are already empty or stale. Third, one credentialed analyst per state could consume the whole pack fee.

## 4. Final call
- **Domain:** one US state's SNAP income and deductions, plus WIC adjunctive eligibility.
- **Buyer:** US nonprofit screening and navigation organizations (#05-type); #06-type integrators later.
- **Delivery:** self-hosted container or in-browser library, no data to the vendor, fixed-fee services. First deliverable: an independent second implementation diffed against PolicyEngine and the state's worksheets, plus an annual change-impact report. UK second.
- **Stop:** the hosted API, metering and OpenFisca export, and the "named reviewer" language until a real reviewer exists.

**Verdict changed?** Still no purchase, but harder: no evaluation, even free, until the shipped package computes and a benchmark exists.
