# 18 - VC / market analyst report

## 1. Verdict
As built, this is a research artifact, not a product: 15 federal SNAP rules, zero users, nothing for Europe. Buyers pay for current, signed-off rules in their jurisdiction, not a "compiler". I would not invest or buy today; one paid pilot earns a second meeting.

## 2. Assumptions challenged
- **H1 (hosted API hard sell):** Half right. No stored household data makes privacy solvable. The real problem: a self-hosted Apache-2.0 engine is free, so self-hosting is table stakes, not revenue. Charge for content and obligations.
- **H2 (pay for currency/sign-off):** Agree, but it makes this a services business with consultancy margins. The named signer cannot be a part-time HR-operations employee; you need a credentialed attorney or policy expert.
- **H3 (private first):** Agree on direct government sales. But private benefit-eligibility buyers are few and grant-funded. Real money is tax/payroll content, where incumbents (Vertex, Avalara, Thomson Reuters, Wolters Kluwer) own the moat.
- **H4 (EU AI Act):** The runtime is probably not an "AI system" (human-authored rules; recital 12 and the Commission's 2025 guidelines, as I recall). The LLM authoring step and Annex III 5(a) (public-benefit eligibility) will still draw lawyer questions. High-risk timing faced a proposed Digital Omnibus delay; verify.
- **H5 (differentiator):** Provenance, sign-off and "unknown" are features a rival copies in weeks. PolicyEngine (broad US coverage), OpenFisca, Catala, Blawx, Oracle Policy Automation and generic rules engines already sit here. Your export target, OpenFisca, cannot represent "unknown", which undercuts your pitch.
- **Why now:** Cheap LLM authoring destroys translation labour as a moat and creates one around verified, maintained, liability-backed content. Apache-2.0 code is not the moat. The product should exist only as the production line of a rule-pack service.

## 3. Required to sell
- **[buyer must-have]** Coverage of the buyer's real jurisdiction. Federal-only SNAP yields no real determination; states administer it with options. Demo currency: how fast you absorb the October SNAP adjustment; check what 2025 SNAP legislation changed in your fixture.
- **[buyer must-have]** Named, credentialed human sign-off per rule pack, plus professional-liability (E&O) cover.
- **[buyer must-have]** External benchmark against gold rules or an independent calculator. Your 22/22 mutation score and 16/16 citations are self-graded.
- **[legal]** A contracting entity (US LLC or UK Ltd), written confirmation your employer has no IP claim, tax paperwork (W-8BEN-E).
- **[legal]** If hosted: GDPR Art. 28 DPA and a transfer mechanism (Pakistan has no EU adequacy decision, to my knowledge).
- **[procurement]** SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001 if hosted; GovRAMP/StateRAMP for US states (name may have changed). Self-host: SBOM, signed releases, disclosure policy, support SLA.
- **[procurement]** Accessibility conformance (VPAT/Section 508; EN 301 549 in the EU public sector) for the reviewer app.

## 4. Not required / wasteful
- Akoma Ntoso, LegalRuleML, Catala exports before a customer asks.
- More diagnostics, hash-chain and mutation-testing polish.
- API-key and metering features before any paying user.
- Format breadth: ingest only the first customer's corpus.
- Anti-rubber-stamping as a headline: no real reviewer measured.

## 5. Deal-breakers
- No European content: Europe is unsellable today.
- No named qualified signer, no liability cover.
- Unclear IP, no legal entity.
- Federal-only coverage, no benchmark, no user.
- One part-time founder: no SLA, key-person risk.

## 6. Commercial (estimates, low confidence)
- **Equity:** I would not lead. After 2+ paid pilots and a renewal, a small pre-seed cheque. Realistic outcome: profitable niche, not venture scale.
- **As a buyer:** fixed-fee pilot roughly USD 10-30k; annual maintained rule pack with named sign-off and change alerts roughly USD 5-25k per jurisdiction. Per-call API pricing is worth little.
- **Funding:** grants (NLnet/NGI, Sovereign Tech Agency, civic-tech funders; check 2026 availability) beat VC.

## 7. Top 3 changes
1. **Sell before building.** One buyer, one state (benefit-screening vendor or nonprofit; or HR/benefits rules, where you have domain credibility). Eight-week fixed-fee pilot with state options and an October-update demo. Freeze verification features.
2. **Make verified content the moat.** Pay a named benefits attorney as reviewer. Publish a benchmark: cost per verified rule vs hand-coding, real-reviewer catch rate. Incorporate, clear employer IP, buy E&O.
3. **Do not raise; bootstrap and partner.** Pilots plus grants; offer PolicyEngine or OpenFisca a provenance/review layer. For Europe, ingest legislation.gov.uk XML first (one language, structured, point-in-time), not EU-wide Akoma Ntoso.
