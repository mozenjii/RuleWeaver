# RuleWeaver: closing judgment

**Confidence caveat.** Every participant is simulated and every price a guess; their agreement (nine of twenty final calls name the UK) is correlated, not market proof. Only the code facts in 00 and my repo check (no UK content exists; facts 3, 4, 7 open) are verified.

## 1. Needed? Will anyone pay?
**Yes, narrowly: as a change-impact and independent-verification service for teams that already run benefits calculators. No as an engine, compiler or "signed packs". No at all unless a real buyer commits money within 90 days.** Confidence: low; one small paid pilot is reachable, anything recurring is unproven.
- **Strongest for:** change-impact is the one purchase driver named by many unrelated roles (#01, #04, #06-#09, #11, #12, #18); nobody refused it, and #01's anchor (displacing 0.25-0.5 of an engineer) is real budget logic.
- **Strongest against:** all 20 said they would not buy today, the engine is priced $0, the first price offered is a free shadow run, and PolicyEngine covers SNAP and UC free. Every priced pilot needs things that do not exist: signer, entity, insurance, jurisdiction coverage, a working gate.

## 2. The first segment
- **Buyer:** UK private benefits-calculator vendors and advice-sector software integrators (the #09 type), not councils or DWP.
- **Jurisdiction/domain:** England; Universal Credit (UC) core plus the April uprating.
- **Delivery:** self-hosted pack and test vectors, founder with zero access to personal data, fixed-fee services contract (not a licence), no hosted API. A named, insured UK welfare-rights reviewer via a UK partner; founder never signs.
- **Price shape (simulated):** free shadow run on synthetic cases; then a GBP 8-25k fixed-fee pilot; then GBP 10-25k a year for a dated pack plus April impact report. Engine GBP 0. After reviewer pass-through the first pilot roughly breaks even: a reference, not a living.
- **Why UC beats one-state SNAP:** structure, not the circular vote.
  1. The UK buyer is a private vendor with a live calculator as oracle and a fixed April event; the US nonprofit spends restricted grants (#02: "a design partner, not the market").
  2. legislation.gov.uk is one language, structured, point-in-time; US state manuals are HTML/PDF.
  3. No Pub 1075 or offshore-work clauses; UK B2B services are reverse-charged (#17).
  4. The SNAP asset is weaker than it looks: 6 of 10 parameters empty, rules possibly predating the 2025 law (unverified).
  - **UC's cost:** no UK code exists, and #09 names the April deadline against a day job as his own deal-breaker. SNAP stays the demo corpus and day-60 fallback.

## 3. Requirements, sequenced
**(a) Before any outreach (free, days)**
- Legal: employer's written release on moonlighting and IP. Refusal ends the plan.
- Legal: delete claims the code cannot back: "named reviewer", "reviewed", "not an AI system", Annex III/Art 14/Art 22 text, seeded-fault catch rates. Label SNAP "structure only"; say the audit trail "detects casual edits, not an administrator" (FTC Act s.5; facts 1, 3, 7).
- Buyer: the two-page sample (weeks 3-4), SECURITY.md.
- Not yet: entity, insurance, SOC 2, pentest.

**(b) Before a signed LOI**
- Buyer: a free shadow run on the buyer's own synthetic cases with a parity result; sourced, dated parameters inside the review gate.
- Procurement: a UK partner committed in writing to supply the reviewer at a price the fee covers; a broker's written answer on insuring a Pakistan-based sole proprietor.
- Legal: one-page services contract (English law, liability cap at 12 months' fees, B2B only, zero data access); verify the legislation.gov.uk reuse licence; Pakistani tax registration, tested payment rail; 50% deposit.

**(c) Before first paid production use**
- Procurement: insured contracting party; Cyber Essentials if asked (verify); SBOM, signed release; backup maintainer.
- Legal: counsel-reviewed intended-purpose and non-reliance text replacing ADR-021/022; pseudonymised reviewer IDs; AGPL warning on the OpenFisca export.
- Buyer: parity on their cases; an April update SLA you can meet.

## 4. 90-day plan (8-10 evening hours a week; 12 Oct 2026 to about 10 Jan 2027). The calls and shadow run are the real validation.
- **Weeks 1-2, gates.** Request the employer release in writing. Claims cleanup (docs only), SECURITY.md, one broker query. List 25 UK prospects and 3 US nonprofits.
- **Weeks 3-4, sample.** Hand-author a handful of UC provisions from legislation.gov.uk with sourced before-and-after parameters for one past uprating; compare with PolicyEngine-UK; run the existing diff/impact code. No ingestion. *Deliverable:* two-page sample report.
- **Weeks 5-7, conversations (release in hand).** Send 25 personal notes with the sample; aim for 8 calls. Ask how they detect change today, what an uprating costs in person-days, who owns the budget, whether they would run a free shadow run. Ask 2 welfare-rights organisations for a reviewer price; call the 3 US nonprofits as a control.
- **Weeks 8-10, shadow runs.** For anyone who agrees: their synthetic cases, a parity result. Build nothing else.
- **Weeks 11-13, convert and decide.** Written scope, fixed fee, deposit, reviewer cost passed through, lawyer-checked contract.

## 5. Kill criteria
- **Day 30:** employer refuses or will not answer in writing (stop paid work); or under 5 hours a week for three weeks.
- **Day 60:** fewer than 8 calls; or fewer than 2 prospects willing to run a shadow run; or a broker refuses cover and no UK partner will front you. Pivot once, to US one-state SNAP conversations; if those also refuse a shadow run, stop.
- **Day 90:** no completed shadow run with a parity result; or no written fixed fee with deposit or LOI; or the only offer is $0. Stop the paid path; keep it as open source or a grant project (availability uncertain).

## 6. What to tell the founder
1. Nobody has asked to buy this and every price in the simulation is a guess, so treat the next 90 days as a test of whether one real company will pay.
2. The idea worth testing is narrow: telling a UK benefits-calculator maker exactly what a change in Universal Credit law, such as the April increase, does to their numbers, linked to the law.
3. Before you talk to anyone, get your employer's written permission and delete every claim in the project that the code cannot yet back up.
4. Do not build more, form a company or buy insurance until a real person agrees to run your work on their own test cases, and never sign off on the law yourself.
5. If your employer says no, or by day 90 no one has agreed to a paid fixed-fee pilot with a deposit, stop selling it and keep it as an open-source project.
