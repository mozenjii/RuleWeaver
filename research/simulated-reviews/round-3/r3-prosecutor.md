# Round 3 - Prosecution: nobody pays enough, soon enough

All prices are simulated estimates from role-played participants, not market data.

## 1. What they said they would NOT do today
- All 20 round-1 participants: "would not buy today" (#01-#20). Round 2 hardened it: r2-#02 withdrew even a free evaluation; r2-#11 withdrew its free proof of concept; r2-#08 will not pilot until the gate works and an external benchmark exists; r2-#06 called its own $40-80k "a price the founder cannot yet earn"; r2-#04 cut its price to $30-80k/yr.
- Nobody pays for the engine: Apache-2.0, priced $0 throughout (r2-#12, r2-#13).
- The product contradicts its claims (00 facts 1, 2, 3, 6, 7): faults never seeded, "edit" broken, 6 of 10 SNAP parameters empty, NOTICE cites a "named reviewer" who is nobody.

## 2. What they said they WOULD do: conditional, often $0
- Free shadow runs: #01, #02, #03, #11. r2-#19: "the realistic first price may be zero."
- Every pilot price assumes state/UK/EU coverage, a benchmark, a credentialed signer, an entity and insurance. None exist (r2-#14, r2-#15). r2-#08: the $10-40k band "looks like one shared prior".

## 3. Weakest "will pay" claims
- **Grant-funded:** #02 spends restricted money (r2-#02: "a design partner, not the market"); #10 offers grants, not purchases; #18: buyers "few and grant-funded".
- **Free alternatives:** PolicyEngine covers SNAP (#02, #06) and UC (#09); OpenFisca and Catala (#12).
- **Big numbers unreachable:** #03's $50-150k/yr needs tax ingestion, a CPA signer and IRC 7216 handling (discounted by r2-#02, #11, #16, #17). #04's $40-120k needs SOC 2 and 6-12 months of bank diligence. #12's GBP 20-50k makes the founder "a subcontracted engineer" (r2-#08).
- **Sign-off paradox:** buyers with budget keep the signature (#02, #04, #06, #10, #11; r2-#18); buyers without analysts lack budget and cannot self-host (r2-#09).

## 4. Prices do not cover costs
- Year-one chassis about $12-43k (legal $5-15k #15; pentest $5-20k and insurance $2-8k #16; summed in r2-#17, r2-#18) against pilots of $5-25k (#16, #15, #19; #09 GBP 8-15k).
- Reviewer: $150-400/hr means a $20k pilot buys 50-130 hours (r2-#06). Revenue share collides with lawyer fee-sharing rules (r2-#07, r2-#15).
- Cash: net 45-60 plus 5-10% retainage (#06).
- Ceiling, in their words: "one or two pilots a year" (r2-#01); "consulting income, not a company" (r2-#06).

## 5. Conditions the founder cannot meet
- **Credentialed signer:** HR operations is not a credential (#01, #03, #09, #12, #17).
- **Insurability:** a Pakistan-based sole proprietor may be uninsurable (#16, #17, r2-#15). Regulated buyers want an entity before a pilot (r2-#04, r2-#05, r2-#09, r2-#11).
- **Employer IP release:** the "largest legal risk" (#17); not in hand.
- **Geography:** Pub 1075 and offshore clauses bar US public work (#02, #05, #06); EU public buyers can exclude a non-EU bidder (r2-#10); "Europe is unsellable today" (#18).
- **Time and continuity:** April uprating deadline against a day job (#09, r2-#17); bus factor one is "disqualifying" (#10, #20).

## 6. Strongest counter-evidence I could not knock down
1. **#09 (UK calculator vendor):** GBP 8-15k pilot, GBP 10-25k/yr, live oracle, recurring April event, one-language structured law. r2-#05, #10, #13, #14, #15, #16, #17, #19, #20 independently name it most credible; r2-#08, #12, #16 describe an insured UK partner supplying reviewer and contracting paper. Caveat: needs unbuilt UC coverage; r2-#09 now wants a free run first.
2. **Change-impact is a convergent purchase driver:** #01, #06, #08, #11, #12 (r2-#11, r2-#18); r2-#08: "the one thing I would buy." #01's anchor (displacing 0.25-0.5 of a compliance engineer) is "the best pricing logic" (r2-#19). Nobody refused it.
3. **Conversion path:** #01 and #03 would move from a passed shadow run to a fixed fee. One $10-25k pilot within 12 months is not implausible. "Nobody will ever pay" fails; "enough to sustain this founder against costs, a day job and unbuilt prerequisites" is the defensible charge.
