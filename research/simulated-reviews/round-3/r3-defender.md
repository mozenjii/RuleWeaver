## Where the prosecutor wins

- **No real evidence.** All 20 said "would not buy today". The $10-40k cluster is shared priors (r2-#01, #08, #19); nobody was asked. My "twelve of nineteen" is simulated participants reading each other: circular.
- **First price may be $0.** #01, #02, #03, #11 offered only free shadow runs; #02 and #11 then withdrew even that until the gate works (r2-#02, #11).
- **Every price is conditional on unbuilt things:** signer, entity, insurance, non-SNAP content, a computing package, a working gate (facts 1-3, 6, 7).
- **Sign-off paradox.** Buyers with budget keep the signature (r2-#04, #07, #08, #12, #18); buyers who want it lack budget, and reviewer cost eats the pilot (r2-#17).
- **Unonboardable.** A Pakistan-based sole proprietor may be uninsurable (#16, #17); charities, regulated firms, EU public bodies and govtech primes reject one (r2-#04, #05, #06, #09, #10, #11).
- **One veto.** If the employer refuses the IP release, the case ends.
- **Domain split.** #09's buyer is UC, #20 says SNAP; PolicyEngine covers both free (#02, #09, #20). A full-time job versus the fixed April deadline is #09's own deal-breaker.
- **Ceiling.** At best consulting income from "one or two pilots a year" (r2-#01, #06, #18); #20: the tool wins only on re-review.
